## Post 1: the launch

The honest cold-start post. It leads with the weakness, which is the only shape
of self-promotion that works, and the ask is small and specific.

**Hook (the pre "see more" opener):**

> I built a review site for Curtin units. It currently has almost nothing written
> on it, which is the honest problem.

**Body, ready to paste:**

I built a review site for Curtin units. It currently has almost nothing written
on it, which is the honest problem.

curtinhonestly.com. Every time I picked units I did the same thing: read the
handbook, learn nothing useful from it, then ask around and hope I happened to
know someone who had done it. The handbook tells you what a unit covers. It does
not tell you the group assignment is worth 40% and the groups are allocated
randomly, or that the exam looks nothing like the tutorials. The students who sat
it know all of that, and every semester it gets lost in group chats.

So the site is a place to put it. You leave a review of a unit you have done, with
a rating out of five, a workload score out of ten, whether there was an exam,
whether you would take it again, and your grade if you want to share it. You can
compare units side by side before you enrol.

The state of it: the catalogue is real and the content is not there yet. A review
site with no reviews is just a website. So this is less "look at my thing" and
more that if you have two minutes and a unit you have opinions about, that is
genuinely the whole thing it needs right now.

Worth saying up front:

Reviews are posted anonymously. Verify with a student.curtin.edu.au address and
your review carries a verified badge, but your name and email are never shown next
to it.

Reviews get removed for abuse or for breaking the terms. Not for being negative. A
one star review with reasons attached is the most useful thing on the site.

There are no ads and nothing to buy. I pay for it.

Independent student platform. Not affiliated with Curtin University.

If there is something that would make you actually use it, tell me and I will look
at building it.

## Post 2: what "honest" and "anonymous" actually mean here

The mission post. It is the one that earns the right to make the ask in post 3,
and the anonymity paragraph is doing the real work.

**Hook:**

> Two words on that site are doing a lot of work, so here is exactly what each of
> them means.

**Body, ready to paste:**

Two words on that site are doing a lot of work, so here is exactly what each of
them means.

Honest. Every review on curtinhonestly.com is written by a student who took the
unit, and nobody at the University approves what gets published or can take a
review down for being unflattering. Reviews come down for abuse or for breaking
the terms, and that is the entire list. The negative ones are the point. A unit
with a four star average and a nine out of ten workload is telling you something
real, and it is exactly the thing you cannot find out before you enrol.

Anonymous. Your name and email are never shown next to a review. Other students
see only whether the reviewer is a verified student.

Here is the part I would rather say plainly than have you find out later: reviews
are still linked to your account in the database. That is what lets you edit and
delete your own reviews, stops one account reviewing the same unit twice, and
makes it possible to investigate an abuse report. It is not anonymous to the
database. It is anonymous to other students, which is the thing that matters when
you are deciding whether to be candid about a unit you are still enrolled in. All
of it is written out on the privacy page.

The reason to build it this way is that a review system people do not trust gets
used dishonestly or not at all, and both of those produce the same result, which
is a page nobody can act on.

Independent student platform. Not affiliated with Curtin University.

curtinhonestly.com

## Post 4: how it is built

The build story. Lowest stakes of the four, and the one most likely to land with
this particular audience.

**Hook:**

> The interesting part of building a review site is not the reviews. It is that
> one student has to run it.

**Body, ready to paste:**

The interesting part of building a review site is not the reviews. It is that one
student has to run it, on a student budget, and it still has to be up at 9am on
results day.

curtinhonestly.com is an Angular front end served as static content from Azure
Static Web Apps, a Spring Boot API on Azure Container Apps in Australia East, and
Postgres behind it. Images come from a container registry, and GitHub Actions
deploys both halves with OIDC rather than long-lived secrets. Dev and prod are
separate all the way down.

Everything the marketing plan leans on is already in the product rather than in a
spreadsheet: student email verification and the verified badge, anonymous posting,
the workload and would-take-again and grade fields, unit comparison, unit
requests, review reporting, a profanity filter, and a banner that opens on its own
for the two weeks after results because that is the only fortnight most students
are motivated to write anything.

That last one is my favourite piece of the build. The date window lives in the
code, not in my calendar, so the product knows when its own season is and I cannot
forget it.

Independent student platform. Not affiliated with Curtin University.

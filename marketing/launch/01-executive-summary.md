# CurtinHonestly Semester 2 launch: executive summary

Prepared 20 October 2026 for a campaign that opens the day after exams end.

## The one-paragraph version

CurtinHonestly is live, covers every Curtin unit (1,761 across five faculties),
has a working prize-draw and referral mechanic, and has 47 reviews. The product
problem is empty pages, and the one moment each semester when students want to
write about a unit is the fortnight after they finish it. This campaign rides
that moment with a "rant or rave" angle: you just finished the unit, say what it
was actually like. It runs in two phases keyed to the published Curtin calendar,
goes out through the computing and engineering societies first, and is measured
by reviews written, not impressions. Target: 150 reviews on the site by
16 December, with at least three faculties non-empty.

## Goal

|---|---|---|---|
| Reviews on site | 47 | 150 | The backlinks plan's tier 2 (student media) unlocks at roughly 180 reviews; 150 puts tier 2 in reach for Semester 1 2027 |

| Verified student signups during the campaign | n/a | 150 | Each verified account is a potential reviewer next semester too |

## The angle: rant or rave

End of semester is the only time most students have a strong, specific opinion
about a unit and nothing to do with it. Curtin's own survey (eVALUate) captures
some of it privately. CurtinHonestly is where the rest goes, in public, for the
students choosing that unit next.

Public copy keeps both halves visible: "rate it, rant about it, or recommend
it". The ranting energy lives in the hooks and in the Discord posts. The site's
own banner stays neutral, which keeps the platform on the right side of its own
moderation rules (staff may be named only about their teaching, nothing about
private life, appearance or protected attributes).

Because the brand is the word "honestly", nothing in this package invents a
review. Posters use fill-in-the-blank prompts instead of testimonials. The
"zero reviews" poster labels its faculty rows as illustrative.

## Channels, in order

Taken from the SEO backlinks plan (`seo/curtinhonestly.com-audit/findings/backlinks.md`),
which already sequenced them by what a visitor sees when they click through.

1. **Tier 1, now: Curtin student societies.** ComSSA first (the audience that wrote the existing 47 reviews, a live website with a Resources menu, an open-source site repo, a contact address that invites collaboration). Then the Curtin Engineers Club (curated Links page, open-source, actively maintained), the Curtin Data Science Association, and the Curtin Robotics Club. Each gets its own referral slug, its own email and its own Discord post, never two on the same day.
2. **Tier 1, same fortnight: Devon's own channels and the subreddit.** LinkedIn (three posts across the campaign), Instagram (feed plus stories with the 9:16 images), r/Curtin with disclosure that Devon built it.
3. **Tier 1, physical: posters and flyers.** Three designs, each with its own QR and slug, placed in pairs so location is controlled (see the A/B plan). Campus poster rules must be checked with the Guild and Curtin Properties first.
4. **Tier 2, at roughly 180 reviews: student media.** Grok Magazine's web edition is dormant, so the realistic tier-2 move is a Guild newsletter mention or a Guild social post. The press note is written so it can go to Grok's editor address and the Guild comms team in December if the numbers justify it.
5. **Tier 3, not this campaign: press.** Campus Review and Perth press need about 500 reviews and a data story. December's results post is the first draft of that story.

## Success metrics and how they are read

- **Primary:** reviews written during the campaign, by referral slug and by faculty. Read in the admin Campaigns page (signups and reviews per link) plus a weekly count of reviews by faculty.
- **Secondary:** verified signups, unique units reviewed, scans per poster design (visits per slug), conversion from scan to signup to review.
- **Channel verdicts:** which society delivered, which poster design won, whether r/Curtin was worth the moderation load. Each verdict feeds the Semester 1 2027 plan, which should be a repeat with the losers cut.
- **Weekly check-in:** every Monday during the campaign, ten minutes: total reviews, reviews by faculty, visits and reviews by slug, open moderation reports, posters still up.

## Risks and the honest answers

- **Empty pages on click-through.** Unavoidable at 47 reviews. The copy says so and makes being first the point.
- **Rant energy crossing into defamation or harassment.** The moderation runbook in `02-launch-runbook.md` sets a same-day triage rule during both phases and rehearses the staff-naming rule.
- **The prize draw.** Western Australian trade-promotion conditions must be checked before a prize is announced. This is a check, flagged in `05-giveaway-plan.md`; it is not legal advice.
- **Posters removed.** Campus boards are policed. Get permission first, log exposure days, and expect to re-post twice.
- **Thin campus traffic after 13 November.** Posters go up in exam week two, and the December set targets the library, the Guild building, student housing and the Hub, which keep traffic in December.
- **Three stale consistency claims** (who can post, what verification means) will be found by the first sceptical student. They are the first items in the runbook.
